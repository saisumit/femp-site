For most DTC brands, ads are the largest variable cost after the products themselves. They're also the cost most likely to be overstated by the tool reporting it. Meta and Google each attribute sales to their own ads with their own windows and models, so the same order can show up in both reports. Neither knows what your store actually took in.

You don't need a full attribution platform to catch the expensive mistakes. You need a daily check of spend against real sales, and a rule for what to do when it looks wrong.

● In short
  • Track blended ROAS (store revenue ÷ total ad spend) or MER every day. It can't double count.
  • For each ad set or campaign, compare spend with the sales of the products it promotes.
  • Set a floor (for example ROAS under 1.0 for three days) and a rule: cut, pause or move budget.

Platform ROAS versus blended ROAS

Platform ROAS is what Ads Manager shows: revenue the platform attributes to its ads, divided by spend. Useful for comparing ads on the same platform, unreliable as a total.

Blended ROAS, sometimes called MER (marketing efficiency ratio), is all store revenue for the day divided by all ad spend. It's crude, but it's real money on both sides of the line.

blended ROAS = store revenue ÷ (Meta + Google + other ad spend)

Watch the trend. If spend rises 30% and blended ROAS falls from 3.1 to 2.2, the extra spend isn't buying much, whatever each platform says.

Know your break-even ROAS

A ratio of 1.0 means you got your ad spend back in revenue, not profit. To break even on a sale you also need to cover the product, shipping, payment fees and returns:

break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin before ads

If a product keeps 50% of its price after goods, shipping and fees, you need 2.0× just to break even on the first order. Repeat purchases can justify going lower for new customers, but decide that on purpose.

A daily rule that stops slow leaks

  1. Every morning, check yesterday's spend by campaign against product sales.
  2. Flag anything under break-even for three days in a row. One bad day is noise.
  3. Check stock before you scale a winner. Doubling the budget on an item with four days of cover just buys a stock-out (see days of cover).
  4. Look for broken basics: a disapproved ad, a pixel that stopped firing, a landing page that's out of stock.
  5. Decide: cut, pause, or move the budget to what's working.
