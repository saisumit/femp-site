You're owed money in your invoices. Here's where it's hiding.

You already earned this money. It leaks out a few dollars at a time through net payouts, carrier surcharges, 3PL invoices and retailer deductions that nobody has time to check.

By the Femp team · November 15, 2026 · 2 min read

Brands spend a lot of effort winning revenue and very little checking that they keep it. The leaks are small per line: a $12 surcharge, a misapplied fee, one pallet billed that isn't there. Added up over a year, they're often a few percent of revenue, and nobody at a small brand has the job of finding them.

● In short
  • Payouts: match each Shopify payout and Amazon settlement to orders, fees and refunds.
  • Carriers: claim late-delivery refunds and dispute wrong surcharges. Audit firms report recoverable errors of 1–5% of parcel spend.
  • 3PLs: check storage, picks and fees against your rate card. Storage is the line most often inflated.
  • Retailers: if you sell to Walmart, Target or Amazon Vendor Central, dispute invalid deductions inside the window.

1. Payouts that don't add up

Shopify and Amazon pay you net: sales minus fees, refunds, chargebacks, reserves and adjustments. The deposit looks plausible, so nobody checks it. Once a week, take each payout and confirm the orders, fees and refunds in it add up to the amount received. On Amazon, look for fees you can't tie to an order, such as FBA removal or disposal fees, storage charges and inventory adjustments, and for lost or damaged FBA units that haven't been reimbursed.

2. Carrier invoices

UPS and FedEx invoices are long and full of accessorial charges. The common recoverable items:

  • Late deliveries on guaranteed services, where the carrier's money-back guarantee applies (check your contract; some suspend it).
  • Address-correction fees on addresses that weren't wrong.
  • Dimensional weight billed on dimensions larger than your box.
  • Duplicate charges and surcharges your contract waives.

Also watch your shipping cost per order week to week. In 2026 carrier list rates rose about 5.9%, but surcharges rose faster, so the real increase for many DTC shippers was higher. If the residential surcharge share is climbing, it may be time to renegotiate or change services.

3. 3PL invoices

A 3PL bill mixes storage, receiving, picks, packaging and special projects. Compare it with your rate card and the 3PL's own warehouse data:

  • Storage: pallets or bins billed versus actually occupied.
  • Picks: pick fees versus orders shipped.
  • Returns and receiving: charges for work that didn't happen, or fees not in your contract.

Audit firms report that first audits commonly find errors, with storage the most inflated line. Even if your 3PL is honest, a manual billing process makes mistakes.

4. Retailer deductions

If you sell wholesale to big-box retailers, they deduct for shortages, late shipments, labeling and compliance issues, often automatically. Industry estimates say deductions take 2–5% of gross revenue for suppliers, and a large share are invalid. Match each deduction to the bill of lading and ship notice, and dispute inside the window; the proof that wins a shortage claim is usually a signed BOL.

A weekly money check

  1. Match last week's payouts and settlements.
  2. Scan carrier invoices for late deliveries and surcharges; file claims.
  3. Compare the 3PL invoice with your rate card and order count.
  4. List new retailer deductions and their dispute deadlines.
  5. Chase wholesale invoices past due.
