Most chargebacks are lost to a buried email. Here's how to win them.

A chargeback you don't answer is a chargeback you lose: the order, the product and a fee. Most small brands lose them not because the case was weak, but because the email got buried.

By the Femp team · November 15, 2026 · 2 min read

When a customer disputes a charge with their bank, the money is taken back from your payout right away, and you're charged a dispute fee (on Shopify Payments in the US it's $15). You then have a fixed window to respond with evidence. Miss it and the dispute is decided against you automatically.

Industry estimates put the average merchant win rate around 30%, and lower for manual, rushed responses. Plenty of disputes are winnable: the package was delivered, the customer used the product, or they forgot they bought it. What loses them is time.

● In short
  • Answer every dispute. Unanswered ones are automatic losses.
  • Match your evidence to the reason code: delivery proof for "not received", product page and policy for "not as described".
  • Watch your dispute ratio. Visa and Mastercard monitoring programs start at roughly 1% of transactions, and fines follow.

Where disputes show up

In Shopify admin under Orders → Chargebacks, and by email to the store owner. The email is the problem: it lands next to supplier invoices, app notifications and customer messages, and the deadline is in the body, not the subject line. Put every new dispute on a list with its due date the day it arrives.

Evidence that wins, by reason

ReasonWhat to include
Product not receivedTracking showing delivery to the shipping address, delivery date, signature or photo if you have it, matching billing and shipping details.
Not as describedScreenshot of the product page at time of purchase, your return policy, and any messages where you offered a return or replacement.
Fraudulent / unauthorizedAVS and CVV match, IP location, the customer's order history with you, account login, and any emails they sent after ordering.
Subscription canceledSign-up terms they agreed to, renewal reminders sent, and proof no cancellation request came in before the charge.
Duplicate / incorrect amountBoth orders showing different items or dates, or the receipt matching the amount charged.

Write a short, factual cover note: what was ordered, when it was delivered, what the customer said afterwards. The person reviewing it at the bank has a few minutes per case.

Prevent the next one

  • Use a recognizable descriptor on card statements, so customers don't dispute a charge they don't recognize.
  • Answer "where is my order?" fast. Many "not received" disputes start as an unanswered support email.
  • Add signature confirmation on high-value orders.
  • Review risky orders before shipping: mismatched billing and shipping, or customers who've disputed before.
  • Offer an easy refund. A refund costs less than a lost dispute plus the fee.

Keep an eye on the ratio

Card networks track disputes as a share of your transactions. Cross their thresholds and you enter a monitoring program with fines, and your payment provider may hold funds or close the account. A small brand with a bad month can get close quickly. Check your rate monthly.
