How to get your products found on Google and in ChatGPT

Shoppers used to type "rose serum" into Google. Now many of them ask an AI assistant "what's a good rose serum for dry skin under $50?" If your product isn't in the answer, it isn't on the shelf.

By the Femp team · November 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Search is splitting in two. Classic Google results still send most of the traffic, but a growing share of product research starts in AI assistants: ChatGPT shows product cards with prices and links, Perplexity has shopping answers, and Google puts AI Overviews above the blue links. They all pull from the same places: your product pages, your structured data, merchant feeds, and what other sites say about you.

The good news for small brands: these answers reward clear, specific information more than a big ad budget. A well-described product from a small store can be the best answer to a narrow question.

● In short
  • Write titles and descriptions that answer who it's for, what it does and how it differs, using the words shoppers use.
  • Give machines clean data: Product structured data, a Google Merchant Center feed, GTINs, price and availability.
  • Get mentioned elsewhere: reviews, comparison articles and "best of" lists are where AI answers find you.

Test where you stand

Pick five questions a customer would actually ask, specific ones like "best fragrance-free lip oil for sensitive skin" rather than your brand name. Then check:

  1. Google: do you appear on the first page, in Shopping results or in the AI Overview?
  2. ChatGPT and Perplexity: does the answer mention your brand or link to your product?
  3. Amazon and other marketplaces: where do you rank for the same words?

Write the results down. Re-run the same questions monthly. It's the simplest findability score there is.

Product titles that match questions

"Aurora" tells a shopper and a model nothing. "Aurora Rose Face Serum for Dry Skin, 30 ml, Fragrance-Free" answers four questions at once. Lead with what the product is, then the attribute people filter on (skin type, size, material, use). Keep the brand voice in the description, not the title.

Descriptions that answer, not just sell

  • Open with one plain sentence: what it is and who it's for.
  • List the facts a comparison needs: ingredients or materials, size, how to use, what it's not suited for.
  • Add a short FAQ with real customer questions from your inbox and reviews.
  • Write image alt text that describes the product, not "IMG_2041".

Give machines clean data

  • Product structured data. Most Shopify themes output it; check with Google's Rich Results Test that price, availability, brand and reviews are present.
  • Google Merchant Center. Connect it through the Google & YouTube app on Shopify to appear in free Shopping listings. Fix disapprovals promptly.
  • GTINs and consistent names across Shopify, Amazon and your feed, so systems can tell it's the same product.
  • Don't block crawlers you want. If your robots.txt or a security tool blocks OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot or Googlebot, those engines can't read your pages.

Be talked about

AI answers lean heavily on third-party sources: review sites, Reddit threads, gift guides and comparison articles. A few honest mentions on sites that already rank for your category do more than a dozen posts on your own blog. Send product to writers who cover your niche, and reply to reviews so there's a visible record.

Publish content that answers buying questions

A blog post that answers "how to choose a face serum for dry skin" with a clear recommendation is the kind of page that gets cited. Write for the question, not for a keyword count, and link to the products that answer it.
