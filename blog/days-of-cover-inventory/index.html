Your best seller is about to sell out. This number warns you first.

"40 left" sounds fine until you notice you sell nine a day and your supplier needs three days. The number that matters is how many days your stock lasts.

By the Femp team · November 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Stock-outs rarely happen because nobody looked at inventory. They happen because somebody looked at a count, saw a reasonable-sounding number, and moved on. Forty units of your best seller feels like plenty. At nine a day it's four days, and if your supplier needs three days plus a weekend, you've already missed the window.

A stock-out costs more than the lost sales. Your ads keep spending on a product nobody can buy, and on Amazon a stock-out can cost you ranking that takes weeks to win back.

● In short
  • Days of cover = units available ÷ average daily sales.
  • Reorder when days of cover falls below supplier lead time + shipping + a safety buffer.
  • Count Shopify and Amazon FBA together if they draw on the same supply, and pause ads on items about to run out.

How to calculate days of cover

Take the units you can sell now (on hand, minus anything reserved for open orders) and divide by how many you sell per day.

days of cover = available units ÷ units sold per day

For "units sold per day", use a recent window, usually the last 14 or 28 days, so a spike from one viral post doesn't distort it. If you're running a promotion, use the promotion's pace, not last month's.

Set a reorder point from lead time

Days of cover becomes useful when you compare it with how long a restock takes:

reorder when days of cover < supplier lead time + transit + safety days

  • Lead time is from PO to goods leaving the supplier. Use what actually happened last time, not the quote.
  • Transit includes receiving at your warehouse or 3PL. For Amazon, add FBA check-in time, which can stretch in Q4.
  • Safety days cover a slow supplier or a sales spike. A week is common for a steady seller; more for anything imported.

With a 21-day lead time and 11 days of cover, you're already ten days late. That is the alert worth getting: a date and an action, not a count.

Shopify and Amazon together

If you sell on both, look at each channel's stock and its own pace. FBA stock can't fill a Shopify order unless you use Multi-Channel Fulfillment, and your 3PL stock can't fill an Amazon Prime order. A product can be fine on Shopify and two days from running out on Amazon. Count inbound shipments separately: units on a truck aren't available until they're checked in.

Stop advertising what you can't sell

The quietest stock-out cost is ad spend. When an item hits zero, or gets close enough that you'll sell out before the restock lands, its ads keep running. Check ad spend for any product under a week of cover and decide whether to pause, cut the budget or move it to an item with plenty of stock.

Checklist

  1. Calculate days of cover per item, per channel, every morning.
  2. Keep each supplier's real lead time next to it.
  3. Alert when cover drops below lead time + transit + safety days.
  4. Draft the PO at that point, and chase the ETA.
  5. Check ad spend on anything under a week of cover.
